WHAT THE COMPUTER SEES

Your hand becomes data.

The camera feed is processed with OpenCV and MediaPipe. Hand landmarks provide the information needed to identify finger states and track the hand's location within the image. That information is then converted into a control mode and target position.

THE MACHINE

Vision only matters if something moves.

Three servo channels convert the software output into mechanical action. Separate axis assignments make horizontal movement, vertical movement and the firing mechanism independently controllable.

X Arduino D3 → horizontal servo
Y Arduino D5 → vertical servo
Z Arduino D6 → trigger servo

UNDER THE TABLE

The wiring behind the software.

UART links the Raspberry Pi to the Arduino while a shared ground creates the electrical reference needed for reliable communication.

Pi TX → Arduino RX
Pi GND → Shared GND
Arduino D3 → X Servo
Arduino D5 → Y Servo
Arduino D6 → Z Servo
Pi GPIO17 → Laser Signal

FROM BUILD → WORKSHOP

Then I turned the project into something other students could build.

The turret became the centerpiece of a hands-on Raspberry Pi workshop through the Mechatronics Course Union. Students were introduced to the complete connection between software and hardware rather than only being shown a finished robot.

The workshop covered the difference between microprocessors and microcontrollers, Raspberry Pi GPIO, UART communication, Arduino PWM, servo integration, computer vision, gesture interpretation and physical calibration.
